          Behind H-ABM

          Humanized Agent-based Models: a Framework

          Version 3 · 11 September 2025 · Preprint

          From data to urban patterns

          On this table, each agent of a synthetic population follows a recorded day of activities and trips. Occupancy and flows add up those same records: people present in places, and trips between places.

          The layers show these records as they are. They do not show the reasoning behind them.

          Research scope

          Version 3 of the paper proposes the framework. It does not show that the agents exported to this app use its modules, and it does not offer a predictive validation of this population.

          A routine or a walking animation does not establish that an agent has memory, emotions or individual reasoning. Which modules are active depends on the implementation that runs.

          Behind the vote

          A New Town Scale Consensus: Propagational Voting in Urban Decision Making

          Yasushi Sakai · MIT doctoral thesis · 2025

          How it works · pp. 51–62

          Each person starts with one divisible vote. Shares can be allocated to proposals, trusted people or groups, and policy categories. Delegated shares follow the recipients’ recorded allocations through the network, ending at concrete proposals.

          The model uses an absorbing Markov chain to calculate proposal support. It can also analyze the influence of people and intermediaries. A support distribution does not by itself determine which policy is adopted.

          In this example, the trusted person directs their received share to Option B. The housing category divides its received share equally between Options A and B. Only the original person’s vote is traced.

          Pilot evidence · pp. 97–133

          The thesis explores the mechanism through a Boston urban-planning survey and participatory workshops. These studies examine how people express preferences, delegate, and influence proposal support. They provide exploratory evidence at small civic scales.

          Limits & open questions · pp. 141–147

          Scale, voter identity, privacy, intermediary design, and concentrated influence remain open research questions. The mechanism does not guarantee agreement or prevent power concentration.

          Closed delegation loops with no route to a proposal require explicit handling; the thesis discusses this exception on p. 68.

          Related publication

          Propagational Proxy Voting

          Yasushi Sakai · Parfait Atchade-Adelomou · Ryan Jiang · Luis Alonso · Kent Larson · Ken Suzuki

          • arXiv preprint · April 2025
          • Social and Information Networks
          • DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2504.13641
          The idea: when groups combine

          One vote, split into fractions, can go to proposals, to other people, and to “sets containing proposals and participants”: groups and categories that bring them together.

          This is where it becomes most interesting. A group passes on what it receives, so trust in people and priorities between proposals combine across layers, as a party shares its votes among its members. In the paper's words, “intermediary nodes, such as categories or groups, play a significant role in collective decision outcomes.”

          Open questions

          How a group should pass on what it receives is still open, and the calculation grows costly at large scale.

            Behind the conversation

            Conversational Spatial Analytics in Augmented Reality

            City Science · MIT Media Lab · Project contact: Matti Gruener

            The research concept

            The project explores voice-based access to spatial data in augmented reality. An agent retrieves relevant information in near real time and presents a visualization anchored to the surrounding space.

            Supported interactions

            Available in this app now: specific commands that show or hide layers, select an agent and draw their route, and open the research guides. They are typed in the conversation until the voice assistant is connected.

            The project's overview describes specific queries and filtered views, and outlines future support for open-ended questions. This app does not answer open-ended questions.

            Open questions

            Does this improve learning, insight, speed, or access to complex data? Can it support meaningful policy change? These remain research questions.
